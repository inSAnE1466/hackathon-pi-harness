Claims Privacy Harness on BRANE

Team Gezira · MAIN Summit 2026 · AI-Z Challenge Track · Maastricht
Omar · Frederick · Hussein  |  fictional insurer Maasverzekeringen N.V.  |  synthetic data only  |  code: omar/ in hackathon-pi-harness
Employees use frontier AI on insurance claims, sensitive data never leaves the company, and a human makes every decision. BRANE stops data leaving; our harness makes the AI do useful claims work inside those limits, and proves it with a gate test suite and a leakage-guarded evaluation.

In plain words (1 page)

  1. The problem. Insurance staff want to use AI for claims, but claims contain private data: names, bank numbers, health, police reports. Sending that to an outside AI (like ChatGPT) is not allowed under EU privacy law.
  2. Our solution. A safety layer: every message first passes BRANE (AI-Z's "security guard") and our own second check. Together they decide: send to the outside AI, send with names hidden, keep it on the company's own AI, or block it. A human always approves the result; the AI never decides or sends anything itself.
  3. We proved it. It passes all 13 security tests, 3 times in a row, including "if the guard is down, nothing goes out".
  4. We tested it fairly. 1,500 practice examples, plus three locked final exams, each used only once and written by a different author: elderly customers, customers writing in Arabic, and farm insurance, a topic never seen before. The system could not have memorised the answers.
  5. The result. On the new exams BRANE alone let 21-31% of sensitive cases slip out. With our second check added: 0 in Arabic and about 2 in 100 on farm claims for health, crime and decision cases. Correction: names are not yet fully protected: in 15 of 40 (Arabic) and 15 of 36 (farm) cloud-bound claims with a person, BRANE masked some names but not all. A harness guard halves this; a full fix (our local model lists the names and the harness masks them before BRANE) is the next step.

Words used in this report

BRANEAI-Z's AI firewall: the "security guard" between our app and every AI model. It detects private data and decides where a message may go.
Our classifierA second check we built: it asks the company's own AI "which kinds of private data are in this text?". It can only make the decision stricter, never looser.
PlaceholderA code that replaces private data before it leaves, e.g. "Marieke Visser" becomes PERSON_REF_1. The real name is put back afterwards, inside the company.
Local / own AIThe company's internal AI model. Nothing sent to it leaves the company.
LeakPrivate data reaching the outside AI when it should have stayed inside. One leak fails AI-Z's security gate.
Over-blockA harmless request that was kept inside or blocked anyway. Safe, but it makes the tool less useful.

Details for the jury

1. Problem

BusinessClaims handlers want AI to summarise claims, check coverage and draft letters. Claims are full of names, IBANs, BSNs, health details (sick leave, operations, a child in hospital), police reports, and decisions with legal effect. Today insurers either block AI (slow, and staff use personal chatbots anyway) or send everything to the cloud.
LegalIdentifiers: GDPR Art. 5(1)(c), 25, 32; pseudonymised data stays personal data (Recital 26, EDPB Guidelines 01/2025). Health: Art. 9(1), 4(15), UAVG Art. 30(3). Criminal data: Art. 10, UAVG Art. 31-33. BSN: Art. 87, UAVG Art. 46. Automated decisions: Art. 22, UAVG Art. 40, WP251rev.01, CJEU C-634/21. Life/health pricing: AI Act Annex III 5(c), Art. 14. Discrimination: Art. 9, Charter Art. 21, C-236/09 (Test-Achats). Injection: Art. 32, AI Act Art. 15(5), OWASP LLM01.

2. What we built

Claims handler pastes a synthetic claim and picks a task (intake summary, customer letter, or custom instruction).
▼
Harness: pre-tokenises company references (CLM-…, MV-POL-…, KL-…) and checksum-valid BSNs locally · pre-flight POST /policy/test: unreachable, error or no decision means not sent (fail closed) · endpoint allow-list: the harness can only talk to BRANE, never directly to a model.
▼
BRANE AIF /chat/completions: 25 built-in detectors + our semantic rules, strictest action wins: allow → cloud placeholder → PERSON_REF_1 → cloud → restored local → internal GPU refuse → HTTP 422
▼
Human: X-Brane headers verified (missing → answer discarded) · handler Approves / Edits / Rejects every draft · on 422 the case is escalated with the reason (Art. 22 decision, injection, discrimination, bulk export) · the AI never decides or sends · audit log stores claim hash, route, headers, policy fingerprint, reviewer, decision (no claim text).

5. Evaluation design: understanding, not keyword copying

Splits kept apart on four axes dev 800 (tune, train) · val 500 (choose) · test 200 (held out, one run per predictor) · optional 50-row human label audit of test. Test differs from dev in author (separate fragment bank, never saw dev or the policies), vocabulary (Half B terms only: ICD-10 / WHO-ATC derived, e.g. lithium, knee reconstruction, UWV), population (65+ vs working-age) and entities (disjoint names, IBAN banks, ID ranges). Test sha256 42b87bc53972df68… recorded before any run.
Checked, not assumed eval/check_leakage.py: no Half B term in tuning material, no shared IBAN / claim number / email, no shared 8-word sequence between held-out and tuning rows, no elderly scenarios in dev (PASS). 45-59% of sensitive dev/val rows contain no keyword at all; tricky negatives (a dog's therapy at the vet, a Turkish restaurant flooded) test over-blocking.

Predictors (same 14 labels, same deterministic label→route code): keyword rules (the floor) · TF-IDF + logistic regression · own LLM classifier on BRANE's local GPU (A: one call, B: one call per label; a local-only draft policy guarantees nothing leaves) · BRANE policies · combinations (stricter route wins). Metrics: route macro F1, label macro F1, leak rate (local/refused rows sent to cloud), identifier leaks, over-block (benign rows not sent to cloud), cost and latency, slices.

7. Findings

0. Held-out headline: defence in depth. On the held-out test (unseen vocabulary, 65+ population, separate author) the pre-registered winner, BRANE with policy v1-nokw, leaked 21.6% of sensitive rows although it leaked 0% on val: every leak was health-type (care homes, guardians, unseen conditions and medicines). Our harness classifier (one local-LLM call, 14 labels) leaked 5.2%, but only decision / discrimination / injection rows. The errors are complementary, so the pre-registered combination "BRANE + harness classifier, stricter route wins" leaked 1.0% (1 row: a Dutch proxy for refugee status) at 13% over-block. Lesson: val was too close to dev to predict generalisation; the four-axis test exposed it. Round 2 on a 500-row val confirmed this (BRANE alone ~16% leak, combination 0.8%) and selected the combination as the final system. Recommendation: run BRANE plus the harness classifier in production.

1. BRANE semantic keywords act as hard triggers. Every over-block of policy v1 on val was matchedVia: keyword ("Ziekenhuis Maasoever" as a business client, "Operation Hagelschlag", a phishing warning containing "verdachte"). Removing keywords and relying on the GPU judge with good descriptions cut over-blocking on dev from 30% to 0% and raised route F1 from 0.74 to 0.92-0.96.

2. Narrow rules only win without keywords. With keywords, 14 narrow rules (v2) lost to 6 broad ones (more lists, more false triggers). Without keywords v2 scored highest on dev (0.963), but on val v1-nokw generalised better (0 leaks), so v1-nokw was selected.

3. A keyword copier collapses on unseen wording. The keyword baseline leaked 100% of sensitive val rows that contain no guideline keyword; the selected BRANE policy and the LLM classifier did not depend on keywords (slice table).

4. One LLM call beats fourteen. Classifier A (one call, all labels) matched B's route F1 (0.938) with higher label F1, a third of the over-blocking, 1/14 of the calls and ~12x lower latency.

5. More data helps learned baselines, but not enough. TF-IDF + LR went from 0.725 to 0.808 route F1 (dev 200 → 800); leak fell from 38% to 15%, still far from 0: usable as a second opinion, never as the enforcer.

6. The teammate's live policy leaks criminal data. It has no Art. 10 rule: gate test E3 (named suspect, previous convictions) went to the cloud, and it leaked 8.8% of sensitive val rows.

7. An independent model found a gap in our own answer key. Claude Haiku (a different model than the one that wrote our labels) relabelled 50 test rows blind: 82% route agreement, Cohen's kappa 0.73. All 9 disagreements were the same kind: elderly claims mentioning a walker, stairlift, mobility scooter, care-home residence or a court-appointed guardian, which our key treated as not health data. Under a broad reading of Art. 9 (CJEU C-184/20: data that indirectly reveals special-category information is protected) they are health data, and the final system sent 8 of the 9 to the cloud (with names masked): leak on this sample 0% under our key vs 19.5% under the broad reading. Open legal decision for the team's lawyers; we recommend the broad reading and adding assistive devices, care-home residence and guardianship to the health definitions. A second rater (Qwen, different family) could not be used: the live team policy's PROTECTED_TRAIT_PROFILING rule refuses any prompt that merely describes protected traits, including a classification instruction.

8. It generalises to a new language and a new topic, for routing. On two brand-new locked exams the final system sent 0.0% (Arabic) and 1.8% (farm) of health, crime and decision cases to the cloud, versus 21% / 31% for BRANE alone. Correction (payload audit): the "identifier leak" column measures routes, not the text that leaves. Checking BRANE's actual outgoing payloads, a real customer name was still visible in 15 of 40 (Arabic), 15 of 36 (farm) and 6 of 32 (elderly) person-containing rows sent to the cloud: BRANE masks some names in a claim but misses others, more often under load and in Arabic script. A partial-masking guard (classifier sees a name type BRANE did not mask → local) reduces this to 7 / 4 / 4; the complete fix is harness-side name masking before BRANE. This is the most important open item. The keyword baseline collapsed (83-98% leak) and the word-statistics model did not transfer to Arabic at all.

9. Where a legal rule lives matters. Adding the broad indirect-health rule to our classifier halved the elderly leak (31% → 16.5%) without extra blocking of harmless elderly claims (6 vs 5 of 42); putting the same sentence into BRANE's description made BRANE over-generalise (9-10 of 42 harmless elderly claims blocked, minimal pairs 30%). Bias was checked, not assumed.

8. Product gaps observed live (BRANE)

· No native human-approval action (semantic rules only local / refuse); approval lives in agent code.

· No "drop span" action: an unnecessary medical remark forces the whole turn local (T2b).

· Placeholder spans swallow the preceding space (policyholderPERSON_REF_1), slightly degrading cloud answers.

· IBANs and company references are labelled ACCOUNT_NUMBER; category-level policies depend on consistent labels.

· Detector drift: within one evening Presidio began tagging ordinary words ("Which", "Beschrijf") as PERSON (0.85), turning 27 benign dev rows into redacted ones; a Dutch name and BSN were missed in another row (the harness now tokenises checksum-valid BSNs itself).

· PASSWORD false positives on Dutch words ("sleutels" = keys, "inlogcode") turn harmless requests into 422s under a refuse rule.

· Semantic rules judge the whole prompt, including meta-instructions: a classification prompt that lists protected traits is refused as "profiling" (no way to exempt system or harness instructions).

· Arabic-script names are often not detected as PERSON (12% of Arabic rows sent names unmasked without our harness).

· No native Art. 10 category, no volume rule, no per-step rules, injection threshold not configurable, undocumented behaviour when BRANE's own detectors or GPU are down.

9. Limitations

All data is synthetic and LLM-drafted, including the labels; they were validated by scripts and reviewed, not double-annotated by humans. There was no time for a hand-written gold set; a 50-row human label audit sheet (data/audit/) is provided to check the answer key (agreement and Cohen's kappa, scored by eval/audit.py). BRANE's semantic judge varies between runs and its detectors drifted during the evening; comparisons are made within the same time window and the gate suite is repeated. Val was drawn closer to dev than test is, so val overestimated BRANE's generalisation (0% vs 21.6% leak). With only 34 sensitive val rows, "0 leaks" is compatible with a true leak rate of up to ~9% (rule of three); val was therefore grown to 500 rows (dev 800 / val 500 / test 200) for the final decision; the production recommendation (combination) is informed by test and must be confirmed by the human label audit and in live use. Legal citations are to be verified by the team's lawyers before reliance. Results are dry runs (/policy/test); live /chat/completions header evidence follows once the team applies the selected policy.

10. Open items (honest status at submission)

· Name masking: harness-side masking of names the local model finds (so BRANE's partial masking cannot leak them) is designed, not built.

· Enforce the gate in BRANE call #3: today #3 is a second BRANE judgement; the gate's final route should be enforced (block = do not send, local = explicit local model, verify the reply is at least as strict).

· Live policy: the team department still runs a teammate's policy; applying policy/policy_v1_nokw.json and capturing live /chat/completions headers needs the team's agreement.

· Legal review: citations to be verified by the team's lawyers; the broad reading of indirect health (CJEU C-184/20) is our recommendation, not a settled decision.

· Human label audit: the 50-row sheet (data/audit/) is ready; no hand-written gold set was made.

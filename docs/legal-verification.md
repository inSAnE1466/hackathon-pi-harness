# Legal verification

Every legal claim in [research](research.md) and every "Legal or governance source" cell in
`submission/MAIN_Policy_Matrix.csv`, checked against the official text on 2026-11-21. Each row gives the claim, the
provision, a short exact quote, the citation tier and where the text came from, the verdict and the fix. A juror can
open the link in [sources](sources.md) and find the quoted words.

## What a specialist checks that a generalist misses

1. **Which Art. 9(2) point applies, and to whom.** Point (h) is for medicine and care, and Art. 9(3) attaches its
   secrecy rule only to point (h). An insurer needs a national law made under Art. 9(2)(g) or (h). In the
   Netherlands that law is UAVG Art. 30(3)(b), with secrecy in UAVG Art. 30(4).
2. **Art. 22 has exceptions.** The contract exception, Art. 22(2)(a), could allow automated claims decisions.
   What blocks them for medical claims is Art. 22(4), which allows decisions based on health data only under
   Art. 9(2)(a) or (g). A human decision-maker is a design choice that avoids that question. The law does not
   demand it in every case.
3. **Pinpoints shift and texts get amended.** The AI Act was amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 on 27 July 2026.
   Every AI Act pinpoint below was checked against the Official Journal text and the consolidated text of
   27 July 2026.
4. **Annex III 5(c) is about underwriting.** It covers "risk assessment and pricing" in life and health insurance.
   Claims handling is outside it, unless claim data feed underwriting or cancellation.
5. **A case can be cited for more than it held.** Test-Achats is about sex in insurance premiums, not ethnicity.
   SCHUFA's test is "draws strongly". EDPS v SRB was decided under Regulation 2018/1725, and its operative part only
   sends the case back to the General Court; the holdings are in the reasoning.
6. **Guidance status.** EDPB Guidelines 01/2025 are the consultation version, and they are stricter than the Court
   on recipients.
7. **Sector law and codes.** The Dutch insurers' code of conduct (GVPV 2024) does not cover basic health insurance.
   The Dutch supervisory authority's DPIA list names insurers that process health data at large scale.

## Legend

- Tier: `[settled]` is a stable, well-known reference. `[verify]` is real but date- or status-sensitive.
  `[verify-pinpoint]` is an article, paragraph, point or annex number, always checked against the primary text.
- Retrieval: `[EUR-Lex]` (eur-lex.europa.eu HTML), `[OJ consolidated]` (EUR-Lex consolidated AI Act of 27 July
  2026), `[wetten.overheid.nl]`, `[gesetze-im-internet.de]`, `[regulator site]` (edpb.europa.eu, ec.europa.eu,
  eiopa.europa.eu, zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl, verzekeraars.nl), `[vendor site]` (openrouter.ai).
- Verdict: VERIFIED (the provision says it), NEEDS REWORDING (the provision exists but says something narrower or
  different), WRONG (the cited source does not support the claim).

## D. Policy premises in policy/brane.json

The live policy was not changed. Findings:

- `PROTECTED_TRAIT_PROFILING.legalBasis` cites "CJEU C-236/09 Test-Achats" for selecting claims by ethnicity or
  surname. The rule (refuse) is right; the cited case is about sex. The correct basis is Directive 2000/43/EC
  Arts. 2 and 3(1)(h), with GDPR Art. 9(1) and C-184/20.
- `CLAIM_OUTCOME_DECISION.legalBasis` reads as if Art. 22 forbids every automated claim decision. The contract
  exception (Art. 22(2)(a)) could allow some. The refusal stands on Art. 22(4) for health-based decisions, on
  SCHUFA and on the insurer's own policy (POL-5.1). The rule is a lawful design choice, not a wrong premise.
- `HEALTH_DATA` and `CLAIM_HEALTH_CONTEXT` set to local: stricter than the law requires (UAVG Art. 30(3)(b)
  would allow a processor bound to secrecy), not wrong.
